Where everything sits

Build order

LiPo safety. Never let the two battery leads touch. Cut and solder one lead at a time, and connect the battery last, after everything else is wired and checked.

  1. Prepare the printed parts

  2. Print the tray, the lid (red, white, gold), two caps and the pin. Clean the bearing bore and the cap sockets.

  3. Dry-fit the mechanics: press the 608 bearing into the tray hub, slide the pin through, and push both caps on part way. It should spin freely. Take the caps and pin back off.

    The bearing stays in. Pressing it now keeps that force away from any wiring.

  4. Set the 3 brass inserts into the lid pillar tips with a soldering iron, flush with the top of each pillar.

    Heat near finished wiring is a risk, so do all the hot work on plastic first.

  5. Bench test before anything goes in

  6. Export the firmware from Compass Studio and set up the Arduino IDE as its README says. Upload to the loose XIAO over USB-C and watch the Serial monitor.

  7. Clip on the motion sensor, the amp with the speaker, and one LED with short temporary wires. Check that the LED lights, a clip plays, and moving the sensor prints motion.

    Finding a dead board now costs minutes. Finding it inside a closed spinner costs the build.

  8. Check the magnet's pole: power the hall sensor, hold the magnet over its flat face, and confirm OUT drops low. Mark that face of the magnet.

    The US5881 only sees a south pole, so a magnet glued the wrong way up never triggers.

  9. Lid: the lights

  10. Glue the 12 LED boards into the lid pockets, chips facing the windows. A drop of thin CA on the board edge is enough.

  11. Wire the chain in the order on the lid map: DOUT of each board to DIN of the next, and +5V and GND along the chain. Leave three leads about 60 mm long at LED #0 (+V, GND, DIN).

    Those three are the only wires between lid and tray, and the slack lets you open the lid later.

  12. Tray: the electronics

  13. Push the hall sensor into its pocket in the hub wall, flat face up toward the cap, legs out through the wire slot. Solder its three wires and glue it.

  14. Set each part in its guides, as on the tray map: XIAO with USB-C through the rim slot, amp, motion sensor, speaker cone-down over the grille, battery. Double-sided foam tape or a drop of CA holds them.

  15. Solder the wiring in the table, keeping runs short and flat on the floor. Do the battery sense resistors at the XIAO. Bring the three lid leads to D0, 3V3 and GND.

  16. Connect the battery last, one lead at a time. Plug in USB-C: the XIAO's charge light should come on.

  17. Test with the lid off and resting loosely on top: LEDs, sound, and a hand spin.

  18. Balance and close

  19. Add the steel BBs where Compass Studio's balance readout says: 5 for the default build, split between the two slots on the tray map. Spin it on the bearing and adjust before you glue them.

    An unbalanced disc wobbles and stops early. The app works out the count from the real part weights.

  20. Fold the lid leads into the cavity, line the lid's pillars up with the 3 bosses, and lower it on. Drive the 3 M2 × 6 flat-head screws up from the bottom into the inserts. Snug, not tight.

  21. Caps and final test

  22. Glue the magnet into the top cap's pocket, marked (south) side down, toward the disc. The bottom cap's pocket stays empty.

  23. Slide the pin through the bearing. Press the top cap on from above and the bottom cap from below until both seat on the bearing's inner ring.

  24. Spin it. The segment the cap's pointer stops on should be the one the voice reads. Charge through the USB-C in the rim.

Also on the bench

Soldering iron and fine tip, flux, solder, flush cutters, tweezers, thin CA, double-sided foam tape, a multimeter, a 1.3 mm hex key (comes with the screws). Optional: one 330 Ω and one 10 kΩ resistor.

Known gap: there is no power switch yet. The dark LEDs and the hall sensor still draw about 15 mA together, so a 150 mAh battery runs flat in roughly 10 hours of sitting. Charge before class.
